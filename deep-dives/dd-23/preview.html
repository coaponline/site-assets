For this demonstration, I'll use photos of the F-16 from RAF Waddington, Cobra Warrior, 2025. This Deep Dive is more explaining available tools rather than editing. I don't believe Image Stacking is explained in the Learning Resource area and therefore I'll explain below.

1

Open your photos

TipYou can use these tools in either the Library or Develop Modules.

Open your photos in Lightroom either Library or Develop Modules. Ensure you have the filmstrip visible at the bottom of your workspace.

2

Virtual copies

Virtual Copies create multiple copies of your original image without taking up any additional space on your hard drive. Hence the name Virtual Copies.

  • Right click on the photo in the film strip and select Create Virtual Copy. Do this as many times as you like.

This will make a copy next to the photo you've selected. For this example I had 5 virtual copies with slightly different edits.

This allows you to create various edits of the same image, different crops, B&W etc without affecting the raw file. When you're happy with a specific edit just Save as you normally would or leave them all on display in your film strip.

TipI personally try numerous edits of the same image with about 75% of the edits not really working so I can just pick and progress the edit that is working. The other virtual copies can be deleted.

3

Stacking

The other tool I use here is Stacking. What this does is allows you to select multiple photos in your filmstrip and stack them into a group. You can have as many photos as you like in your Stack.

To see the quantity of photos in a Stack, look for the small number in the White Box on the relevant photo within your filmstrip.

For this example, I have 4 Photos in the Stack.

  • Select the photos or batch of photos you wish to create in a Stack.
Right click›Stacking›Group into Stack

You'll now have those various photos in a Stack. All the previews will disappear at this point.

4

Expand the stack

  • Click the small number in the White Box. This will expand the stack out to the right. You'll see 4 of 4 photos in my Stack.

The other thing you can do is move photos up and down the Stack or move a photo to the top of the Stack. In my case I wanted the Edit (2nd Photo) at the Top of the Stack, therefore right click on that image.

Right click›Stacking›Move to Top of Stack

My Edit is now at the top of the stack and the visible photo when the Stack is collapsed.

Just ask if you have any issues or questions.
