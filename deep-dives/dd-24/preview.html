For this one I've used a sunset photo of the Mi-24 from the COAP Poland trip 2026, with a few different colour settings to show the technique. I'm using the American spelling, color, where Lightroom does.

1

Global Point Color

Open your image in Lightroom, in the Develop module. Scroll down to the Color Mixer panel and click Point Color.

At this stage the panel is grey, with no colours or options. Select the picker tool and pick a colour on your image. This isolates that one colour to edit.

Once you pick a colour, the panel fills with options. Move these sliders one at a time and watch what they do to your selected colour.

  • Hue Shift
  • Sat. Shift
  • Lum. Shift
  • Variance
  • Range
2

Visualize the range

Tick Visualize Range. Your image goes black and white, with only the selected colour showing.

Move the Range slider and you'll see the selected area grow or shrink. Repeat as many times as you need, picking different colours and moving the sliders.

TipYou can also adjust the endpoints and the smoothness, so the change fades in without hard lines. The drop-down arrow on the Range slider gives you three more: Hue Range, Saturation Range and Luminance Range.

3

Color and luminance range masks

You can use the same technique inside Lightroom's Masking tool. Create your mask, then use the same sliders in the masking panels.

Create›Color Range Mask
Create›Luminance Range Mask

Pick your colour or luminance area and move the sliders. The extra here is that you can set the range for luminance and colour, and every tone and colour slider is there too: Exposure, Contrast, Highlights, Temp, Tint and the rest.

It's a little-known tool in Lightroom, and it can make a subtle change or a big one.
